# Proposals and decision records

These documents help a reader assess or understand a choice. Follow an existing repository or team template where applicable, and scale detail to the decision.

For a proposal or RFC, state the problem, recommendation, constraints, alternatives, and consequences. Separate settled facts from assumptions and questions needing a decision. Include rollout or compatibility details when they affect feasibility. State what the reader is being asked to decide.

For an ADR, record the decision and its status, the context that makes it necessary, significant alternatives, and consequences. Preserve the evidence, date, dependencies, and uncertainty needed to interpret the record later. A rejected alternative needs enough explanation to prevent repeating the same debate; it does not need the drafting history.

Keep operational rationale even when it concerns process. For example, "The import must finish before switching readers because both formats share one index" belongs in a migration decision. Commentary about how the assistant drafted the record does not.

Use coherent paragraphs for reasoning and lists or tables for genuinely comparable alternatives. A short decision can remain short; do not fill sections with invented detail. Finish by checking whether a reader can identify the choice, why it fits the constraints, and what obligations or unresolved risks follow.
