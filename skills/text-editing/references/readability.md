# Readability evidence

First ask whether the intended reader can find the point, understand its conditions, and take the appropriate action. Readability scores do not establish these properties.

Sentence length and dense passages can help locate difficulties. Flesch, Flesch-Kincaid, Gunning Fog, and SMOG rely on word or syllable estimates and are commonly used for English text. Do not apply English-language thresholds indiscriminately to other languages. Technical identifiers, code, tables, abbreviations, and short samples can distort results.

If measurements are requested, use an appropriate available method, state what text was measured, and distinguish estimates from conclusions. If no suitable measurement tool is available, explain the limitation and provide qualitative findings. A writing review does not require installing a tool.

Low sentence-length variation can indicate monotonous prose or a perfectly usable sequence of instructions. Inspect the actual text before joining or splitting sentences. Connector density is not a target to optimize.

When comprehension is uncertain, a representative reader can attempt the documented task or paraphrase the main point. Use the observed confusion to improve the text. Do not treat a mandatory reader study as a prerequisite for every publication.

For a review, examine the main message, organization, undefined terminology, and evidence before sentence statistics. If the reader must act, check whether the required action and its conditions are identifiable. Dense noun strings, weak transitions, or repeated explanations can remain confusing even when a formula gives a favorable score.

A reader check can ask someone to paraphrase the main point, identify the required action, and point out where they became uncertain. Use the result to diagnose the draft, not to force a fixed sentence or paragraph count.

See [source guidance](sources.md) for review methods and their applicability limits.
